Mirroring changes digit appearance and reverses the horizontal motion component; rotation changes appearance and turns each direction by $90^\circ$. Both preserve speed, so the speed histogram should remain largely unchanged while direction frequencies may change.

Speed-up retains existing frames at larger intervals. It preserves digit shape but increases displacement between observations.

Together, these provide useful controlled comparisons: mirroring/rotation test appearance and orientation changes at unchanged speed, while speed-up tests larger movements at unchanged shape. However, mirroring and rotation do not isolate appearance from motion direction.
